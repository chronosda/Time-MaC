\subsection{Robustness Under Missing Observations}

To further assess the robustness of Time-ME under incomplete observations, we conduct an ablation study under two missing-data settings: random mask and block mask. In both cases, we compare the full model with coupled-Mamba fusion (CM) against its counterpart without coupled-Mamba (no-CM). The mask ratio is varied from 0.2 to 0.5 for random masking, and from 0.1 to 0.5 for block masking. Since this study focuses on robustness under corrupted inputs, we report both MSE and MAE for compact comparison.

Table~\ref{tab:missing_ablation} shows a clear trend. Under random masking, the CM variant consistently outperforms the no-CM baseline from the 0.2 missing ratio onward, and the performance gap gradually enlarges as the missing ratio increases. For example, when the missing ratio reaches 0.5, the MSE is reduced from 0.40632 to 0.38791 and the MAE is reduced from 0.48721 to 0.47265. Under block masking, the difference is marginal at the 0.1 level, but from 0.2 onward the CM variant again remains consistently better, with the advantage becoming more pronounced under heavier corruption.

These results complement the clean-setting observations in the main experiments. While coupled-Mamba does not necessarily provide uniform gains under standard forecasting conditions, it yields clear robustness benefits when the input information is severely degraded. This suggests that the main value of coupled-Mamba lies in information-limited scenarios, where cross-view compensation and structured multimodal coupling can better recover useful predictive signals.
